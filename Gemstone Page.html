Here are some fun facts

Bismuth

  • Bismuth is more of a metal than a crystal since it's a brittle, crystalline, white metal with a slight pink tinge.
  • The staircase-shaped pyramid formations are called "hopper crystals."
  • Hopper Crystals happen when the outer edges of the crystal grow much faster than the inner center as the liquid metal cools, leaving the structures beautifully hollow.
  • The color is an optical illusion.
  • Light bounces off the top and bottom of this thin oxide silver layer, scattering into a spectrum of blues, purples, golds, and pinks the exact same physics that creates a rainbow in a soap bubble or oil slick.

Jade

  • Jade is not a single mineral; it actually refers to two completely different minerals: nephrite and jadeite.
  • Nephrite is more common and softer, while jadeite is rarer, harder, and can command much higher prices.
  • Various cultures often associate jade with healing properties, and it's believed to promote health, longevity, and prosperity.
  • In traditional Chinese medicine, jade is thought to cool the body and is used to treat a variety of ailments.
  • While diamonds are harder (scratch easier), jade is made of an interlocking, tightly woven meshwork of microscopic needle-like crystals that absorb shock and resist shattering.

Benitoite

  • Benitoite is an ultra-rare, deep-blue gemstone famous for its diamond-like fire and glowing fluorescent properties.
  • It can be mistaken for sapphire, but it is more valuable.
  • Under shortwave ultraviolet (UV) light, benitoite fluoresces a vivid, bright sky-blue.
  • Designated as California's official state gemstone in 1985, gem-quality benitoite is found almost exclusively in San Benito County, California.
  • While natural benitoite comes in blue, colorless, or pink, rare brownish-yellow stones can sometimes be heat-treated to turn orange, though heating carries a high risk of the crystal shattering or exploding.

Kunzite

  • Kunzite was first discovered in 1902 in California by the gemologist George Frederick Kunz, chief jeweler for the renowned Tiffany & Co. Named in honor of Kunz's contributions to mineralogy.
  • High-profile stars like Oprah, Scarlett Johansson, and Lady Gaga have all worn statement kunzite pieces at major public events.
  • Kunzite can fade and lose its vibrant pink color when exposed to direct sunlight or high heat for too long, nicknamed the "evening stone" or "midnight stone."
  • Kunzite can glow or linger with light after being exposed to ultraviolet (UV) light or bright sunlight in darkness.
  • A famous 47-carat kunzite ring was originally purchased by John F. Kennedy for Jackie Kennedy, which later sold at a Sotheby's estate auction for over $400,000.
