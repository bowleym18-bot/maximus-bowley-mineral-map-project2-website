Here are some fun facts

Gold

  • Gold is the only naturally yellow metal on Earth; other metals can turn yellowish, but only after they oxidize or react with chemicals.
  • Gold is a noble metal, meaning it doesn't rust, tarnish, corrode, or lose its shine when exposed to air.
  • Astronaut helmet visors are coated with a microscopic layer of gold to reflect intense infrared solar radiation and glare.
  • The average human body contains a tiny, trace amount of gold, about 0.2 milligrams.
  • Gold is the most malleable metal known; a single ounce can be beaten into a paper-thin sheet covering about 100 square feet, or stretched into a wire 50 miles long.

Silver

  • Silver ranks number one among all elements for conducting electricity and heat.
  • Silver atoms are forged deep inside stellar explosions and supernovae.
  • Ancient people used silver vessels and utensils to keep water and food from spoiling, giving rise to the phrase "born with a silver spoon in your mouth."
  • Silver has had more commercial and industrial patents issued for its use than all other metals combined.
  • Alchemists used to refer to silver by the name 'lunar' because it was associated with the moon, sea, and various lunar goddesses.

Lead

  • On the scorching mountain peaks of the planet Venus, the surface temperatures are high enough to vaporize certain minerals, causing metallic lead sulfide and bismuth sulfide to condense and "snow" down on the peaks.
  • Medieval alchemists believed lead was the oldest metal and spent centuries trying to transform it into gold.
  • Lead melts at just 621 Fahreheit (327 Celsius), which is a very low temperature for a metal, making it super easy to melt down and shape.
  • Lead-acid car batteries make lead one of the most heavily recycled materials in the world, with a huge percentage of global lead coming from melted-down old batteries.
  • The "lead" inside your pencil is actually a harmless form of carbon called graphite.

Titanium

  • Titanium matches steel in strength while being roughly 45% lighter and significantly stronger than aluminum.
  • Titanium resists rust and saltwater corrosion so well that it is used in marine equipment and nuclear waste containers.
  • The average human body contains about 20 milligrams of titanium.
  • Roughly 95% of mined titanium is turned into titanium dioxide, a brilliant white powder used in sunscreen, paint, paper, cosmetics, and toothpaste.
  • Because human tissue doesn't reject it, titanium is the go-to metal for artificial hip joints, dental implants, and bone pins.
